## STAR Method Reminder

```
S - SITUATION: Set the context (30 sec)
    "When I joined HPE, there was no AI assistant..."

T - TASK: Your responsibility (15 sec)
    "I was tasked with building the system end-to-end..."

A - ACTION: What YOU did specifically (45-60 sec)
    "I designed a hierarchical multi-agent architecture..."

R - RESULT: Quantified outcome (30 sec)
    "The system now serves 6M devices with 50% MTTR reduction..."
```

## Power Phrases to Use

**Starting your answer**:
- "Let me give you a specific example from my work at HPE..."
- "This reminds me of a challenge I faced with Copilot..."
- "I have a relevant experience — let me walk you through it..."

**Showing trade-off thinking**:
- "Let me think about the trade-offs here..."
- "There were two approaches I considered..."
- "The decision came down to [X] vs [Y]..."

**Demonstrating ownership**:
- "I owned this end-to-end..."
- "I took responsibility for..."
- "This was my system, so when it failed, I was the one debugging at 2am..."

**Showing learning**:
- "What I learned from this was..."
- "I changed my approach after this experience..."
- "This failure taught me to always..."

**Handling uncertainty**:
- "I didn't have perfect information, so I..."
- "I made the decision to proceed because..."
- "I validated my assumptions by..."

---

## Phrases to AVOID

| Don't Say | Why | Say Instead |
|-----------|-----|-------------|
| "That's easy" | Sounds arrogant | "Here's how I'd approach it" |
| "I don't know" (alone) | Shuts down conversation | "I haven't faced that, but I'd approach it by..." |
| "We always do X" | Sounds inflexible | "A common approach is X, but depending on context..." |
| "Obviously..." | Dismissive | "An important consideration is..." |
| "It depends" (alone) | Evasive | "It depends on [specific factors], for example..." |

---

## Handling Difficult Moments

**If you need time to think**:
> "That's a great question. Let me think about the most relevant example..."
> (5-10 seconds of silence is okay)

**If you don't have a perfect example**:
> "I haven't faced that exact situation, but a related challenge was..."

**If you realize your answer is going off-track**:
> "Actually, let me give you a better example..."

**If they probe deeper than your knowledge**:
> "I'd want to research that more before giving a confident answer. My instinct would be..."

**If you make a mistake in your answer**:
> "Let me correct that — I misspoke. What I meant was..."

## Final Reminders

### DO:
- Be specific — use real examples with numbers
- Own your failures — show what you learned
- Show enthusiasm — genuine interest, not sycophancy
- Pause to think — silence is better than rambling
- Connect to Google — relate your experience to their scale

### DON'T:
- Give generic answers — avoid "I'm a team player"
- Blame others — own your part in every story
- Over-prepare — sound natural, not robotic
- Forget the result — always end with quantified impact
- Rush — better to give one good answer than three rushed ones
