# Google - Googleyness & Leadership

Google's behavioural interview focuses on **Googleyness** - their unique cultural assessment that evaluates how you align with Google's values and work style.

## What is Googleyness?

Googleyness is Google's term for the cultural traits they look for in candidates:
- Comfort with ambiguity
- Bias for action
- Collaborative nature
- Valuing diverse perspectives

## Google's Interview Structure

| Round | Duration | Focus |
|-------|----------|-------|
| Googleyness & Leadership | 45 min | Behavioural questions using STAR method |

## Key Google Values to Demonstrate

### 1. Focus on the User
- Always start with user impact
- Show empathy for end users
- Demonstrate data-driven decisions that improved user experience

### 2. Think 10x, Not 10%
- Show ambitious thinking
- Describe projects where you pushed boundaries
- Demonstrate innovation mindset

### 3. Launch and Iterate
- Embrace iterative development
- Show comfort with shipping MVPs
- Describe learning from failures

### 4. Share Everything You Can
- Collaboration over competition
- Knowledge sharing examples
- Cross-team impact stories

## Sample Google Behavioural Questions

1. **Tell me about a time you had to work with ambiguous requirements**
   - Focus: Comfort with ambiguity, problem-solving

2. **Describe a situation where you disagreed with your manager**
   - Focus: Respectful pushback, data-driven arguments

3. **Tell me about your biggest failure and what you learned**
   - Focus: Growth mindset, learning from mistakes

4. **How do you handle conflicting priorities from different stakeholders?**
   - Focus: Prioritization, communication, collaboration

5. **Describe a time you had to influence without authority**
   - Focus: Leadership, persuasion, collaboration

## Google-Specific Tips

::: tip Key Phrases
- "I focused on user impact by..."
- "The data showed that..."
- "I collaborated with [team] to..."
- "We iterated based on feedback..."
:::

::: warning Common Mistakes
- Being too modest about achievements
- Not quantifying impact
- Focusing on individual work over collaboration
- Not showing learning from failures
:::
