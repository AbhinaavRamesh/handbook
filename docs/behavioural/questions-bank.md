## Company-Specific Focus Areas

### Amazon Leadership Principles Questions

Amazon interviews heavily focus on their 16 Leadership Principles. Expect deep dives into:

| Principle | Example Question |
|-----------|------------------|
| Customer Obsession | "Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer" |
| Ownership | "Describe a time you took on something outside your area" |
| Invent and Simplify | "Tell me about an innovative solution you created" |
| Are Right, A Lot | "Describe a time you made a tough decision with incomplete data" |
| Learn and Be Curious | "Tell me about a time you learned something new to solve a problem" |
| Hire and Develop the Best | "How have you helped develop talent on your team?" |
| Insist on the Highest Standards | "Tell me about a time you refused to compromise on quality" |
| Think Big | "Describe your most ambitious project" |
| Bias for Action | "Tell me about a time you acted without waiting for permission" |
| Frugality | "Describe a time you accomplished more with less" |
| Earn Trust | "Tell me about a time you built trust with a skeptical stakeholder" |
| Dive Deep | "Describe a time you had to dig into details to solve a problem" |
| Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit | "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a decision" |
| Deliver Results | "Describe a time you delivered an important project under pressure" |
| Strive to be Earth's Best Employer | "Tell me about a time you improved a teammate's work experience or well-being" |
| Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility | "Describe a time you considered the broader impact of your work on the community or society" |

### Google Focus Areas

Google emphasizes Googleyness and Leadership. Expect questions on:

- **Emergent Leadership**: Leading without authority, influencing peers
- **Intellectual Humility**: Being wrong, changing your mind
- **Collaboration**: Working across teams, building consensus
- **Ambiguity**: Navigating unclear situations, making decisions with incomplete data
- **Innovation**: Novel approaches, challenging status quo

### Meta Focus Areas

Meta focuses on impact and moving fast. Expect questions on:

- **Impact**: Measurable outcomes, scale of influence
- **Speed**: Quick iteration, rapid learning
- **Openness**: Transparency, feedback culture
- **Building**: Creating things that serve people
- **Bold Moves**: Taking calculated risks

### Microsoft Focus Areas

Microsoft emphasizes growth mindset and customer focus:

- **Growth Mindset**: Learning from failures, continuous improvement
- **Customer Success**: Deep customer understanding
- **Diversity & Inclusion**: Creating inclusive environments
- **One Microsoft**: Cross-team collaboration

## Preparation Checklist

- [ ] Review all categories and identify your strongest story areas
- [ ] Prepare at least 2 STAR stories per category
- [ ] Research target company's specific values and principles
- [ ] Practice out loud with timing (2-3 minutes per answer)
- [ ] Map your stories to multiple question types for flexibility
- [ ] Prepare stories that show both success and learning from failure
- [ ] Have specific metrics and outcomes for each story
- [ ] Practice the curveball questions to reduce surprise factor

## Final Tips

1. **Quality over quantity**: Better to have 10 well-prepared stories than 20 half-prepared ones
2. **Be specific**: Vague answers are forgettable; specific details make you memorable
3. **Own your role**: Use "I" more than "we" - interviewers want YOUR contribution
4. **Show growth**: Even in success stories, show what you learned
5. **Practice timing**: 2-3 minutes is the sweet spot; practice with a timer
6. **Adapt on the fly**: Your prepared stories should flex to the specific question asked
7. **Be authentic**: Interviewers can tell when you're fabricating; genuine stories resonate
8. **Quantify impact**: Numbers make your impact tangible and credible
