# The STAR Framework for Behavioral Interviews

> **Structured approach to answering "Tell me about a time when..." questions**

---

## What is STAR?

| Component | Purpose | Time Allocation |
|-----------|---------|-----------------|
| **S**ituation | Set the context | 20% (~30 seconds) |
| **T**ask | Your responsibility | 10% (~15 seconds) |
| **A**ction | What YOU did | 60% (~90 seconds) |
| **R**esult | Outcomes achieved | 10% (~15 seconds) |

**Total target time: 2-3 minutes per answer**

---

## Situation (20%)

### What to Include
- Brief context: company type, team size, project scope
- The challenge or opportunity that arose
- Why it mattered (stakes involved)

### What to Avoid
- Excessive background details
- Company-specific jargon
- Irrelevant context

### Template
> "I was working as a [role] at a [company type] on a [project type]. We faced [challenge] which was critical because [stakes]."

---

## Task (10%)

### What to Include
- Your specific responsibility
- Why you were the right person
- The goal you needed to achieve

### Template
> "My task was to [specific responsibility] because [reason you were chosen]. The goal was to [measurable objective]."

---

## Action (60%) - THE MOST IMPORTANT PART

### What to Include
- Step-by-step actions YOU personally took
- Your decision-making process
- How you collaborated with others
- Obstacles you overcame
- Technical or strategic approaches used

### Structure Your Actions
1. **First, I...** (initial assessment/planning)
2. **Then, I...** (key action #1)
3. **Next, I...** (key action #2)
4. **Finally, I...** (concluding action)

### Power Phrases
- "I personally led..."
- "I made the decision to..."
- "I convinced the team by..."
- "I identified that..."
- "I implemented..."

### What to Avoid
- Using "we" too much (focus on YOUR contribution)
- Vague descriptions
- Skipping over difficult parts

---

## Result (10%)

### What to Include
- Quantifiable outcomes (metrics, percentages, time saved)
- Qualitative outcomes (team morale, relationships built)
- Lessons learned
- Recognition received

### Quantifiable Results Examples
- "Reduced processing time by 40%"
- "Increased customer satisfaction from 3.2 to 4.5 stars"
- "Delivered 2 weeks ahead of schedule"
- "Saved $50K in annual costs"

### Qualitative Results Examples
- "The approach was adopted as a best practice"
- "I was asked to present the solution to leadership"
- "The client renewed their contract"
- "Team velocity increased in subsequent sprints"

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It's Bad | How to Fix |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| Too long on Situation | Loses interviewer attention | Use timer: max 30 seconds |
| Using "we" constantly | Unclear what YOU did | Replace with "I" and specify your role |
| No metrics in Results | Lacks credibility | Always quantify when possible |
| Scripted delivery | Sounds rehearsed | Know key points, not exact words |
| Negative tone about others | Shows poor collaboration | Focus on what you learned/achieved |

---

## Practice Exercise

### Step 1: List 5 Significant Experiences
Think of times when you:
1. Led a project or initiative
2. Resolved a conflict
3. Failed and learned from it
4. Influenced without authority
5. Made a difficult decision

### Step 2: Draft Each in STAR Format
Use the templates above to structure each story.

### Step 3: Time Yourself
- Full story: 2-3 minutes
- Situation: max 30 seconds
- Action: at least 90 seconds

### Step 4: Get Feedback
Practice with someone and ask:
- Was my role clear?
- Did the result seem impactful?
- Was I engaging throughout?

---

## STAR-L Variation (Add Learnings)

For failure/challenge questions, add:

| Component | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| **L**earning | What you'd do differently |

> "Looking back, I learned that [key insight]. In future situations, I would [improved approach]."
