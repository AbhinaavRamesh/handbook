# 1. Interview Structure at L4

[← Back to Index](./00_INDEX.md) | [Next: ML Domain Round →](./02_ML_Domain_Round.md)

---

Google's L4 Machine Learning Engineer interviews typically span **5-6 rounds across 1-2 days**. Understanding the structure helps you allocate preparation time effectively.

## Interview Rounds Overview

| Round | Focus Area | Duration | Key Skills Tested |
|-------|------------|----------|-------------------|
| Recruiter Screen | Background, motivation, leveling | 30 min | Communication, career narrative |
| Phone Screen 1 | DSA + Coding | 45-60 min | Algorithm design, code quality |
| Phone Screen 2 | ML Fundamentals | 45-60 min | Core ML concepts, math foundations |
| ML Domain Round | Practical ML application | 45-60 min | Applied knowledge, trade-offs |
| Applied ML Round | Deep technical depth | 45-60 min | Production ML, debugging |
| System Design | End-to-end ML systems | 45-60 min | Architecture, scale, monitoring |
| Googliness/Leadership | Culture fit, collaboration | 45 min | STAR stories, teamwork |

---

## L4 vs L5 Expectations

### L4 (Senior Software Engineer - ML)

**What they're looking for:**
- Solid ML fundamentals with practical application skills
- Ability to own features/components end-to-end
- Translate business problems into ML solutions
- Debug and optimize existing systems
- Clear communication of technical trade-offs

**Typical scope:**
- Own a significant feature or component
- Work within established architecture
- Contribute to design decisions
- Mentor junior engineers occasionally

**System design depth:**
- Focus on component-level design
- Understand end-to-end flow
- Discuss trade-offs clearly
- Propose reasonable solutions

### L5 (Staff Software Engineer - ML)

**What they're looking for:**
- Deeper technical leadership
- Cross-team influence and coordination
- Mentorship and growing others
- Drive technical direction for a team
- Navigate ambiguity independently

**Typical scope:**
- Own a system or multiple large components
- Define architecture for new initiatives
- Influence roadmap and technical decisions
- Regular mentorship responsibilities

**System design depth:**
- System-level design with organizational impact
- Consider long-term maintenance and evolution
- Address cross-team dependencies
- Propose solutions considering org constraints

## Round-by-Round Breakdown

### Recruiter Screen (30 min)

**Purpose:** Verify fit, discuss leveling, explain process

**What to prepare:**
- 2-minute elevator pitch of your background
- Why Google? Why ML? Why now?
- Salary expectations (research beforehand)
- Timeline and availability

**Your talking points:**
> "I've spent the last 2 years building the Aruba Networking Copilot from the ground up—a production ML system serving 6M+ devices with hierarchical multi-agent architecture. I'm looking for opportunities to work on ML at Google's scale, particularly in areas like search, recommendations, or infrastructure."

---

### Phone Screen 1: DSA + Coding (45-60 min)

**Format:** 1-2 LeetCode-style problems, typically medium difficulty

**What they assess:**
- Problem decomposition
- Algorithm selection and optimization
- Code quality and correctness
- Communication while coding

**Preparation:**
- Focus on arrays, strings, trees, graphs, dynamic programming
- Practice explaining your thought process out loud
- Time/space complexity analysis for every solution
- Edge cases and testing

---

### Phone Screen 2: ML Fundamentals (45-60 min)

**Format:** Conceptual questions + potential coding

**Common topics:**
- Supervised vs unsupervised learning
- Loss functions and optimization
- Gradient descent variants
- Overfitting/underfitting
- Basic probability and statistics
- Feature engineering basics

**Sample questions:**
- "Explain the bias-variance trade-off"
- "When would you use L1 vs L2 regularization?"
- "How does dropout help prevent overfitting?"
- "Implement logistic regression from scratch"

---

### ML Domain Round (45-60 min)

**Format:** Open-ended ML problem design

**What they assess:**
- Practical application of ML knowledge
- Handling ambiguity
- Trade-off discussions
- End-to-end thinking

**See:** [Detailed ML Domain Round Guide](./02_ML_Domain_Round.md)

---

### Applied ML Round (45-60 min)

**Format:** Deep dive into ML concepts and production challenges

**What they assess:**
- Depth of understanding core concepts
- Production ML experience
- Debugging and optimization skills
- Handling edge cases

**See:** [Applied ML Deep Dive](./03_Applied_ML_Deep_Dive.md)

---

### System Design (45-60 min)

**Format:** Design an ML system end-to-end

**What they assess:**
- Architecture thinking
- Scale considerations
- Trade-off articulation
- Monitoring and maintenance

**See:** [System Design Framework](./05_System_Design_Framework.md)

---

### Googliness & Leadership (45 min)

**Format:** Behavioral interview with STAR-based questions

**What they assess:**
- Collaboration and teamwork
- Handling ambiguity
- Navigating conflict
- Growing yourself and others
- Intellectual humility

**Sample questions:**
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision"
- "Describe a project where requirements were unclear"
- "How have you helped a teammate grow?"
- "Tell me about a time you failed and what you learned"

**Your STAR stories to prepare:**
1. NER context persistence debugging → Problem-solving under pressure
2. Hierarchical agent architecture decision → Technical leadership
3. 98.5% accuracy achievement → Iterative improvement
4. Building Copilot from scratch → Handling ambiguity
5. 50% MTTR reduction → Business impact

## Timeline and Process

```
Week 1:     Recruiter screen → Schedule phone screens
Week 2-3:   Phone screens (1-2)
Week 3-4:   Onsite scheduling
Week 4-5:   Onsite interviews (virtual or in-person)
Week 5-6:   Hiring committee review
Week 6+:    Team matching and offer
```

**Total:** 2-6 weeks from initial contact to offer

## Key Reminders

✅ **Do:**
- Ask clarifying questions
- Think out loud
- Discuss trade-offs explicitly
- Connect solutions to business impact
- Show enthusiasm for the role

❌ **Don't:**
- Jump to solutions without understanding the problem
- Ignore scale and production concerns
- Forget to discuss monitoring and maintenance
- Give one-word answers
- Pretend to know something you don't
